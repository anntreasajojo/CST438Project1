# Project 01 Post Mortem - [07 / CST438 Project 1]

Repo: https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1

## Context
Our team wanted to build a nutrition tracking app that would let users make an account, log in, view their meals by different categories, add food, track nutrition information, be able to save their favorite foods and view charts of their daily and weekly meal logs. What we actually ended up shipping was a working prototype of this app with valid login processes and registration screens, a landing page, breakfast, lunch, and dinner screens, food categories, favorites functionality, a working database, the ability to add foods through the USDA FoodData Central API, and nutrition tracking charts. Some of the features were not completed, such as the reminders feature, the separate snacks screen, the food detail page, and the yearly chart view. However, the main functionality and UI of the app was completed.

## What went well
1. **We split the work into one issue per screen and one branch per person.** The kickoff issues divided the app by screen (landing, login, register, each meal page, categories, favorites, charts) and by database table, and each of us worked on our own `name/feature` branch. This let all four of us build in parallel from the first week. By Sep 10 the landing page, breakfast and lunch screens, categories, the user table and the register page were all merged, and merge conflicts stayed rare.
2. **We added automated checks halfway through the project.** [PR #27](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/27) added PMD, detekt and a GitHub Actions workflow on Sep 14, so every PR after that ran Lint, the unit tests and static analysis automatically. Reviewers could start from a passing check instead of pulling and building every branch; the approval on [#35](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/35) opens with "Gradle check passes and there are no conflicts with main."
3. **Our PR descriptions explained the change.** Most PRs linked the issue they closed and described what was added and what was left out. When the database schema and meal records changed, the PR explained the migration, so the rest of the team could build on the new data without having to ask.

## What went wrong
1. **Large PRs were merged with little or no review.** [#19](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/19) (+1019 lines) was merged 13 minutes after it was opened with no review, [#35](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/35) (+430) after 8 minutes, and [#17](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/17) (+182/−617) after 3 minutes. Of the 20 app PRs we merged, 4 have no approving review. Cause: we never agreed on a review rule or a size limit, and main was not protected, so anyone could merge anything. The CI check was also never made a required status check (PR #27 notes this needs a repo admin, and nobody followed up). On top of that, work tended to land late in the evening before an iteration deadline, when the pressure is to merge rather than to read.
2. **Scope was decided by individuals instead of by the team.** Some features grew past what we had agreed to keep simple for Project 01 (personal calorie targets, the charts page), while other planned work was dropped without a recorded decision (#7 and #8 were closed without being built, and the yearly chart view was cut inside a PR description). The same gap caused duplicate work: the entries table in [#29](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/29) was closed unmerged after the dated meal records in [#37](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/37) covered the same data. Cause: our kickoff issues had short descriptions with no acceptance criteria and no "out of scope" line, so there was nothing to check a PR against. Each of us filled in the details alone while coding, and scope changes happened inside PRs instead of on the issue, where the whole team could see and discuss them.
3. **Environment and build problems cost us several days.** Every build after the first one failed until Kotlin and KSP were upgraded on Sep 9, a kotlinx-serialization version mismatch broke builds again later, and some of us could not run the app on the emulator at first. Cause: we had no written setup steps or pinned toolchain until the README was added on Sep 24 ([#45](https://github.com/anntreasajojo/CST438Project1/pull/45)), and dependency changes were made on feature branches before CI existed, so breakages only showed up on other people's machines after merge.

## Advice to our next teams
1. **Write acceptance criteria and an "out of scope" line on every issue at kickoff, and change scope only on the issue.** If a PR is going to do more or less than its issue says, have that conversation before the code is written, not after.
2. **Set up CI in the first week and protect main.** Require the check to pass and at least one approving review before anything is merged. It takes whoever owns the repo about ten minutes, and it removes the temptation to self-merge at midnight before a deadline.
3. **Get everyone running the app on the emulator in the first week, and write the setup steps down as you go.** A README with the exact Android Studio, Kotlin and API key steps would have saved us several days of "it works on my machine."
